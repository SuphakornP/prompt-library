# Worked example: extending a support pilot

This is synthetic practice material, not a real pilot report.

## Input

User request: "Create a four-slide English storyline for a five-minute update to our operations director. I want approval to extend the support triage pilot for another two weeks with the same three staff."

Supplied notes:

- N1: The pilot ran for two weeks with three staff. It received 12 requests, resolved eight, and has four waiting for manager approval.
- N2: There is no comparable baseline for resolution time or cost. No customer satisfaction survey was run.
- N3: The proposed extension keeps the same three staff for two more weeks. The purpose is to test whether a daily approval review helps clear pending requests.

## Expected output

### Presentation frame

Audience: operations director. Desired decision: approve a two-week extension with the existing three staff. Main message: the pilot resolved eight requests and exposed an approval bottleneck; a limited extension can test an improvement. Four slides fit a suggested five-minute speaking plan, which has not been timed in rehearsal.

### Slide 1: The pilot resolved eight requests; four still await approval

- On-slide content: Two-week pilot; three staff; 12 requests received; eight resolved; four pending manager approval.
- Visual direction: One horizontal bar divided into eight resolved and four awaiting approval, with exact counts labeled.
- Evidence: N1.
- Speaker notes: "This is a count of pilot requests, not evidence that we became faster or less expensive. We do not have a comparable baseline." Suggested allocation: one minute.

### Slide 2: The remaining cases give us a specific process question

- On-slide content: Four requests are waiting for approval. The next test asks whether a daily approval review helps clear them.
- Visual direction: A simple request-to-review-to-resolution flow with the approval step highlighted; label the daily review as proposed.
- Evidence: N1 and N3. There is no evidence yet that daily review improves results.
- Speaker notes: "The open cases point to a place to investigate. The proposed change is a hypothesis, not a proven fix." Suggested allocation: one minute.

### Slide 3: A two-week extension can test daily review without adding staff

- On-slide content: Keep the same three staff. Run for two more weeks. Introduce the proposed daily approval review. Suggested measures: age of pending requests and time from review to decision.
- Visual direction: A two-week test outline with separate rows for the proposed activity and proposed measures.
- Evidence: Scope and staffing from N3; the measures are recommendations in this storyboard, not approved metrics.
- Speaker notes: "Before starting, confirm who can provide daily approval decisions and agree on the measures. We have not established a cost or speed improvement." Suggested allocation: two minutes.

### Slide 4: Approve the extension and confirm approval coverage

- On-slide content: Decision requested: two additional weeks with the current three staff. Confirm the manager coverage needed for the proposed daily review.
- Visual direction: A concise decision box with extension scope and an unresolved approval-coverage item.
- Evidence: N3; approval coverage is an open implementation question.
- Speaker notes: "I am asking for a bounded test, not a wider rollout. If approval coverage is unavailable, we should revise the experiment before proceeding." Suggested allocation: one minute.

### Production questions

Confirm the dates of the extension and which manager can cover the daily review. No satisfaction, savings, or resolution-time claim should be added without evidence. This deliverable is a text storyboard; no slide deck or chart image has been created.

## Observable checks

- The output has four slides and asks for the supplied two-week extension, not a wider rollout.
- Eight resolved plus four pending reconciles to 12 requests.
- No speed, cost, or satisfaction improvement is invented.
- Proposed measures and daily-review effectiveness are not described as established facts.
