# Worked example: onboarding evidence

This is synthetic practice material, not a report about a real organization.

## Input

User request: "Write a short English evidence brief for the product lead. Do these notes show that an onboarding checklist increases activation? Use only the excerpts below."

- S1, Usability sessions, 4 November 2026, paragraph 1: "Eight invited new users tried the current onboarding flow. Six asked which setup step to do next. We did not test a checklist."
- S2, Support review, 7 November 2026, paragraph 1: "Of 18 onboarding support tickets received last week, 11 asked what to do after creating an account. Tickets are not unique users."
- S3, Pilot notes, 12 November 2026, paragraph 1: "Activation was recorded for 35 of 100 sign-ups in the month before the checklist, and 45 of 100 sign-ups in the month after it. These were different cohorts. Marketing changed the acquisition campaign at the same time. The activation definition is not included in these notes."

## Expected output

### Answer and scope

The supplied excerpts suggest that users need clearer next steps, but they do not establish that the checklist caused activation to increase. The recorded activation rate rose from 35% to 45%, a 10 percentage-point increase across different cohorts. A simultaneous campaign change and an unspecified activation definition limit interpretation. This brief covers three excerpts, not the underlying study records.

### Findings and evidence

| Finding | Source and locator | Observation | Limitation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Some tested users were unsure what to do next | S1, paragraph 1 | Six of eight invited users asked about the next step | Small invited sample; no checklist was tested |
| Next-step confusion also appears in support contacts | S2, paragraph 1 | Eleven of 18 tickets concerned what follows account creation | Tickets are not unique users or a sample of all sign-ups |
| Recorded activation was higher after checklist introduction | S3, paragraph 1 | 35/100 before and 45/100 after | Different cohorts, concurrent campaign change, missing activation definition |

### Synthesis

S1 and S2 describe similar confusion in different settings, which supports investigating clearer onboarding guidance. Neither measures a checklist's effect. S3 is consistent with an improvement, but the campaign or cohort differences could also contribute. The ticket count cannot be combined with the session count into a single user percentage.

### Implications and next questions

A limited checklist experiment is a reasonable proposed next step, not a proven conversion intervention. Before evaluating it, confirm the activation definition and compare comparable users over the same measurement window. Ask whether other onboarding changes occurred alongside the campaign. Do not promise a 10-point lift from these notes.
